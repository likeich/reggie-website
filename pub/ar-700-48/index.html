AR 700-48 — Management Of Radiologically Contaminated Equipment Outside The United States

AR 700-48 governs the policies, responsibilities, and procedures for managing radiologically contaminated equipment outside the United States.

AR 700-48 addresses radioactive materials, depleted uranium equipment, risk management, training, handling, decontamination, and radiological surveillance. It requires coordination, safety measures, recurring training, equipment identification, controlled handling, and medical monitoring.

Applies to: This regulation establishes the policies, responsibilities, and procedures for the proper management of radiologically contaminated equipment (RCE) located outside of the United States.

Questions and answers

How long can an approved deviation last?

Deviations may only be approved for 1 year or less. (paragraph 2-4)

What happens to an approved deviation after an accident or mishap?

Any accident or mishap occurring under an approved deviation will automatically terminate an approved deviation until the respective approval authority completes an investigation and revalidates the approval of the deviation. (paragraph 2-4)

Who must coordinate the release of information about potentially contaminated Army equipment or personnel?

Release of information regarding potentially or allegedly contaminated Army equipment and personnel must be coordinated with the Chief of Public Affairs prior to release. (paragraph 9)

What must a trained RSO do with radiologically contaminated equipment during noncombat operations?

During noncombat scenarios or when operations allow, a trained and command-appointed RSO will identify, segregate, isolate, secure, and label all RCE. (paragraph 4)

How often is refresher training required for personnel handling radioactive materials and radiologically contaminated equipment?

Refresher training will be conducted annually, with additional training conducted when needed.
