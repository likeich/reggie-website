AR 195-6 — Department Of The Army Polygraph Activities

AR 195-6 governs Department of the Army polygraph use, examination authority, and procedures for polygraph programs.

Questions and answers

Can someone face adverse action for refusing a criminal investigation polygraph?

For criminal investigations, no adverse action will be taken against persons refusing or unwilling to be examined by polygraph. Other investigation, as appropriate, may continue following the person's refusal to be examined by polygraph. (paragraph 1-6)

What form is required to request a polygraph examination?

The investigative element requesting approval to conduct a polygraph examination will submit a completed DA Form 2805 (Polygraph Examination Authorization) (refer to http://www.apd.army.mil/productmaps/eforms/daforms.aspx for further details) to the concerned person. (paragraph 2-1)

Which outside polygraph examinations can be used in official DOD proceedings?

Only polygraph examinations conducted by Federal agencies or conforming to DOD standards will be used in official DOD proceedings. (paragraph 2-2)

Who may conduct an independent polygraph examination?

Only certified polygraph examiners will conduct independent polygraph examinations. (paragraph 2-2)

What consent is required for a criminal investigation polygraph?

Voluntary consent in writing on DA Form 2801 (Polygraph Examination Statement of Consent) must be obtained from the person being examined if connected with a criminal investigation ((refer to http://www.apd.army.mil/product-maps/eforms/daforms.aspx for further details)). (paragraph 2-2)
