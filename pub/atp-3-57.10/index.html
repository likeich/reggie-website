The publication covers historical applications, authorities, populace control, resources control, dislocated civilian operations, and noncombatant evacuation operations. It addresses measures including curfews, movement restrictions, travel permits, licensing, checkpoints, ration controls, and property control.

Applies to: This publication applies to the Active Army, Army National Guard/Army National Guard of the United States, and United States Army Reserve unless otherwise stated.

Questions and answers

What do rules of engagement typically include?

The ROE authorized for a mission typically include specific instructions regarding the use of force. (paragraph 1-47)

How often must rules of engagement be reviewed?

ROE must be continuously reviewed to ensure that they are clear and lawful, that they are sufficient to address the requirements of the mission, and that they provide the commander with the necessary powers to deal effectively with the threat and security environment. (paragraph 1-48)

What measures are included in populace control?

Populace control measures include curfews, movement restrictions, travel permits, registration cards, and resettlement of civilians. (paragraph 1-22)

What does international law require during populace control?

International law requires the military force to focus on essential tasks that establish a safe, secure environment and address the immediate humanitarian needs of the local populace. (paragraph 2-1)

When will U.S. forces execute resources control measures?

U.S. forces will not execute these measures unless the requirements are clearly beyond the capabilities of the HNs security forces, the HN has requested assistance, and appropriate U.S. authorities (to include the U.S. Ambassador) have granted such assistance. (paragraph 3-11)
